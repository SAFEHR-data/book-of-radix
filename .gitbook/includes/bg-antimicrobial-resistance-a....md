---
title: Background - AMR paragraph
---

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) kills more people than HIV/AIDS or malaria and is worsening. By 2050, an estimated 1.9 million deaths annually will be directly attributable to AMR (GBD 2021 Antimicrobial Resistance Collaborators, 2024, 10.1016/S0140-6736(24)01867-1). In England, the burden is rising; London has the highest rates of antimicrobial-resistant bacteraemia in the country.

Resistance is driven by several interconnected factors: antimicrobial use in livestock for growth promotion and prophylaxis, environmental contamination via wastewater containing antibiotic residues, and human antibiotic consumption. In human health, inappropriate prescribing is a principal driver, both at population and individual level (Thom KA, et al. Clin Infect Dis 2019; 68: 1581–84). An estimated 20% of primary care antibiotic prescriptions in the NHS are inappropriate. Optimising antibiotic use is accordingly a central target of the UK National Action Plan for AMR.
