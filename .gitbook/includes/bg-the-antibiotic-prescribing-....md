---
title: Background - Abx Prescribing Problem
---

Most antibiotics are prescribed empirically, before culture results are available. Results are obtainable in fewer than half of patients and take two to three days to return. In the interim, clinicians make a probabilistic assessment of the likely source of infection — urinary, respiratory, or other — and may broaden treatment to cover multiple potential sources where diagnostic uncertainty exists.

Where a likely source is identified, clinical guidelines exist to standardise care. Guidelines synthesise population-level evidence on treatment effectiveness with local data on bacterial prevalence and resistance patterns, and incorporate stewardship principles such as restricting extended-spectrum agents to limit resistance selection.

In practice, however, guideline adherence is inconsistent. Concordance rates for community- acquired pneumonia (CAP) are as low as 58% (British Thoracic Society audit data). Some deviation is clinically justified — for example, where diagnostic uncertainty is high or where there is evidence of prior treatment failure or known resistance. However, unwarranted deviation risks over- or under-treatment, treatment failure, and the further propagation of antimicrobial resistance.
